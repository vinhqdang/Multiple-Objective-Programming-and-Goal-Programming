Please find attached our manuscript, "Learning Marginal Effects for Goal Programming: Shrinkage-Adjusted Predict-then-Optimize Budget Allocation for Social Goals", submitted as Original Research to the special issue "Multiple Objective Programming and Goal Programming: Artificial Intelligence for Decision Making in Economic and Social Sciences".

The paper studies goal programming for public budget allocation when the marginal effects of spending are learned from data. We formulate predict-then-optimize weighted and Chebyshev goal programs, propose a one-parameter shrinkage of predicted effects, and evaluate it in a reproducible simulation. The main finding is that learning removes most of the gap between equal allocation and an oracle, while the value of shrinkage depends on the goal programming variant: modest gains for Chebyshev goal programming with ample data, and losses for weighted-sum goal programming.

The work contributes to goal programming (formulation and evaluation), to artificial intelligence in decision making (machine learning inside the optimisation model), and to economic and social applications (allocation of budgets across regions for social goals). The study is simulation-based and we state its limitations in the manuscript. The manuscript is original, has not been published, and is not under consideration elsewhere.
