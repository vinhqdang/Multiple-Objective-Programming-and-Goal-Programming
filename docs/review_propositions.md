# Independent review of Sections 2-4 (paper2/main.tex) and docs/08

Scope: forward model, Propositions 1-5, Section 4 (estimation). Notation as in the paper: u_k = alpha_k x_kt - c_k - d_k (upper-edge breakpoint), l_k = alpha_k x_kt - c_k + d_k (lower-edge breakpoint), u_k <= l_k when d_k >= 0. Stationarity (beta_k<0): linear cost sigma = sum_k (p_k - q_k); quadratic cost D = sum_k (p_k - q_k). I re-derived the signs (derivative of the upper term in D is -omega+, of the lower term +omega-).

Numerical checks are in /tmp/review/chk.py (numpy). Results are quoted where relevant.

Summary of verdicts: Prop 1 true for all beta_k<0 but statement/proof incomplete and false as written for a goal with beta_k>0; Prop 2 as worded is wrong (vacuous reparametrisation, and contradicted by Prop 3); Prop 3 correct only if edge periods are labelled; Prop 4 conclusion (not point-identified, generic) is essentially right but the proof's mechanism ("coincident breakpoints") is backwards and uses Prop 1 illegitimately; Prop 5 correct only with extra conditions and a sign fix, and the "point identification" claim is conditional on latent states.

## Issue list

### 1. [critical] Prop 2 (Effective weights): "raw weights and transmission are not separately identified" is false / contradicted by the paper
Location: main.tex Prop 2 and abstract ("only effective weights are identifiable"); docs/08 Prop 2.
Problem: with the sign of beta_k fixed, (beta_k, w_k) <-> (alpha_k, omega_k) is a bijection (alpha=1/|beta|, w = alpha*omega). A reparametrisation cannot lose identifiability. Proposition 3 itself identifies alpha_k (hence |beta_k|) from edge periods, and then w_k = alpha_k omega_k is identified whenever omega_k is. So "only effective weights are identifiable" is wrong. The proof ("the substitution is exact") shows only that the model can be written in new coordinates, not non-identification.
What is true (and what the authors probably mean): (a) all weights are identified only relative to the adjustment-cost coefficient lambda, which is normalised to 1 (this normalisation appears in docs/08 but is absent from main.tex eq. (1)); (b) the sign of beta_k must be known (see Issue 3); (c) alpha_k is identified only if the goal's edge binds in at least two periods with distinct x_kt (Prop 3) and is otherwise only set-identified, and in that case omega_k = w_k/alpha_k and w_k are not separately pinned down. 
Fix: restate as "Decisions depend on (w,beta,tau,b,lambda) only through (alpha, tau, b, omega) where omega = w|beta|/lambda ... (with sign(beta) known). Consequently weights are identified only relative to lambda and in units of the transmission; it is natural to report omega." Delete "raw weights and transmission are not separately identified", or replace by the weaker "when alpha_k is not identified (no binding edge) w_k and beta_k are not separately identified". Add lambda to eq. (1) or state the normalisation explicitly in Section 2 and in the abstract claim.

### 2. [major] Prop 1 (Breakpoints): coercivity argument and breakpoint set wrong for goals with beta_k > 0 and for the direct-control case; "minimiser" vs "the observed decision"
Location: main.tex Prop 1 and following text of Section 2; docs/08 Prop 1.
(a) Scope. Eq. (2) and C_t are derived only for beta_k<0. Section 2 says only "beta_k != 0" and that for alpha=1 "D is replaced by -D". With a goal of the other sign, g_kt(D) = alpha x - c + D, the breakpoints are D = c +- d - alpha x, i.e. the reflection of the stated ones. I tested the literal set C_t = {0} U {alpha_k x_kt - c_k -+ d_k} on random K=2 instances with one goal of positive beta: the grid minimiser was missed in 97/300 instances (gap>1e-4). With the sign-aware set sg_k*(alpha x - c -+ d) (sg_k = +1 for beta<0, -1 for beta>0) it was never missed (0/300, also 0/300 for K<=4 all-negative).
(b) The slope asymptotics in the proof ("tends to 1+sum omega^- ... and -(1+sum omega^+)") are valid only if every beta_k<0. With mixed signs the limits are 1 + sum_{beta<0} omega^- + sum_{beta>0} omega^+ and the symmetric expression. Coercivity itself is trivial and needs no slope computation: F_t >= C(D) = |D| because every goal term is >= 0 (needs omega >= 0). Suggest this one-line proof; it covers all sign patterns.
(c) The proof tacitly assumes omega_k^pm >= 0 and d_k >= 0. If d_k<0 (inverted band) both penalties are active between l_k and u_k; the quadratic candidate set then needs 4 states per goal. State the parameter space (d >= 0, omega >= 0).
(d) "A minimiser lies in C_t" is correctly stated, but Prop 4 and Prop 3 use it as "the observed D*_t is a breakpoint". That does not follow when the minimiser set is a non-degenerate segment (flat optimum; the paper's own motivation for the margin). Only a unique minimiser is necessarily a kink (a point where F' is continuous with F'=0 would force flatness). Fix: state "if the minimiser is unique, it belongs to C_t; in general the set of minimisers is a closed interval whose endpoints belong to C_t". 
(e) Distinctness of breakpoints is not needed for Prop 1 (ties only shrink C_t) - fine, but note it is needed in Prop 4/5 (Issue 6, 8).
Suggested statement: "Let sg_k = -sign(beta_k), g_kt(D) = alpha_k(y_k(D) - tau_k) = alpha_k x_kt - c_k - sg_k D. For omega >= 0, d >= 0 and C(D)=|D|, F_t is convex, piecewise linear, F_t >= |D|, and its set of minimisers is a compact interval with endpoints in C_t = {0} U {sg_k(alpha_k x_kt - c_k +- d_k)}."
(f) Direct-control case (alpha=1, sg=-1). The substitution "D replaced by -D" also changes the meaning of ^+ and ^-: overshoot of the goal now corresponds to D<0, and the slope of the overshoot term in D is +omega^+. All later formulas (stationarity, Prop 3 sign, Prop 5, Applications step sizes) must be re-signed. This is not stated and the Application text ("step down above the band") silently uses it.

### 3. [major] Prop 5 (Gradualism): statement imprecise, sign errors, and "point identification" is conditional on latent states
Location: main.tex Prop 5; docs/08 Prop 5; Section 4/Applications use.
(a) State definitions. The multipliers p_k, q_k are determined by the position of the goal at the optimum D*_t (g_kt(D*_t) vs +-d_k), not at D=0 (the unchanged-policy forecast x_kt). "Exactly goal k is strictly above its band and no edge is reached" is ambiguous. Precise condition: p_k = omega_k^+ iff u_k > D*_t (goal k strictly above at the optimum); for every j != k, u_j < D*_t < l_j (strictly inside, so p_j=q_j=0); and then D*_t = omega_k^+ > 0. In particular "strictly above at D=0" (u_k>0) is not sufficient: if u_k <= omega_k^+ the optimum is D = u_k (edge reached, p_k interior). Check by K=1 closed form: D* = min(omega^+, u) for u>0, 0 for u<=0<=l, max(-omega^-, l) for l<0.
(b) Two (or more) goals outside their bands. D*_t = sum_{above} omega^+ - sum_{below} omega^-. Numerical check (K=2, both goals far above, d=0.5): omega^+ = (0.3,0.2), (0.1,0.4), (0.25,0.25) all give D* = 0.5. Only the sum is revealed. The statement should say so; in the "exactly one goal outside" case with other goals inside, fine, but identification of individual omegas needs single-goal periods or a full-column-rank system (below).
(c) Sign of the second claim: strictly below its band gives q_k = omega_k^-, hence omega_k^- = -D*_t (D*_t<0), not +D*_t. "Similarly" hides this. In the direct-control case (sg=-1) the first claim also flips: omega_k^+ = -D*_t. As stated the proposition is false for alpha=1 budget-share goals (it would give a negative weight).
(d) Mixed with edges. With sg_k general, stationarity is D = sum_k sg_k (p_k - q_k).
(e) The identification statement is conditional on knowing the states, but states are functions of the unknown (alpha, c, d). The correct, sharper formulation: given (alpha, c, d) the state of each goal in each period is determined by the data (g_kt(D*_t) vs +-d_k), and for periods with no goal on an edge the stationarity equation is linear in omega: D_t = sum_{A_t} omega^+ - sum_{B_t} omega^-. Hence omega is point-identified iff the 2K columns of the sign matrix over such periods are linearly independent (single-goal periods are a sufficient special case). The proposition's "when such periods occur" should be replaced by this rank condition, and a separate argument is needed that (alpha,c,d) are identified (labelling, Issue 4). Periods where a goal sits on an edge add only inequalities 0<=p_k<=omega_k^+.
(f) Combining with Prop 3. For omega_k^+ and for the upper edge of goal k one needs two different kinds of periods (strictly above with u_k > omega_k^+, and edge-stopping with 0<u_k<omega_k^+). For a one-sided situation (as in Applications: "almost no ally above the band") omega_k^+ is not identified and only a bound on tau_k+b_k exists; the paper does say this, but the abstract/Prop 5 claim "weights and aspiration levels are both point-identified" should be explicitly conditional on both period types for each side of each goal and on K small relative to the number of regimes.
(g) D*_t = 0 is no longer a kink under quadratic cost; the zero-action zone is u_k <= 0 <= l_k for all k. In this zone nothing is learned about omega or about the interior of the band; this is the dominant regime (inaction) and should be mentioned for identification (it informs only the inequalities u_k<=0<=l_k).
Fix: restate: "Let C(D)=D^2/2, omega>=0, d>=0, sg_k=+1. F_t has a unique minimiser, D*_t = sum_k (p_kt - q_kt) with p_kt in [0, omega_k^+] ... If at D*_t exactly one goal k is strictly above its upper edge and all others are strictly inside their bands, D*_t = omega_k^+; if it is strictly below, D*_t = -omega_k^-. More generally D*_t = sum_{A_t} omega^+ - sum_{B_t} omega^- whenever no goal sits on an edge, and omega is point-identified iff the resulting linear system has full column rank."

### 4. [major] Prop 3 (Edge revelation): requires labelled edge periods; "two + two" is sufficient, not necessary; coincidence of edges
Location: main.tex Prop 3 and proof; docs/08 Prop 3.
Correctness of the algebra: yes, D* = alpha_k x_kt - c_k - d_k on the upper edge; two periods with distinct x_kt give (alpha_k, c_k+d_k); lower edge then gives c_k-d_k; tau=c/alpha, b=d/alpha. I agree.
Gaps: (a) The observer sees (D_t, x_t), not which goal/edge is binding. For K>=2 the equations D_t = alpha_k x_kt - e_k hold with an unknown label (k, upper/lower). The statement "two upper-edge ... observations determine" is a statement about oracle-labelled periods. The identification claim needs either an explicit labelling assumption or a genericity argument that the assignment is recoverable from (D_t, x_t) (not shown; for K=1 it is easy: every nonzero D under linear cost is an upper-edge breakpoint if D>0 and a lower-edge one if D<0 - check, it should be stated). Note in linear cost D*_t>0 can only occur at an upper breakpoint u_k (at l_k>0 the left derivative is +1 from |D|); this helps labelling and should be included.
(b) Once alpha_k is known from the upper pair, one lower-edge observation suffices (not two); with the lower pair alone alpha_k is over-identified (consistency check). Minor but "two and two" misstates the minimal condition.
(c) Time-invariance of (alpha, tau, b) and x_kt exogenous/the same forecast the DM used is assumed; with realised inflation as proxy (Section 5) this is violated - the proposition is a noiseless-oracle result.
(d) Under quadratic cost an edge stop requires 0 < u_k <= omega_k^+ (or the mirror image for l_k<0); the proposition should note the condition under which an edge is reachable, or the case sg=-1.
(e) "distinct x_kt" is needed only for goal k's own x; other goals' x are irrelevant but their breakpoints must not coincide with D_t (else the label is ambiguous).

### 5. [major] Prop 4 (Set identification, linear cost): conclusion plausible but the proof is incorrect on the key point; "generically never point-identified" not proven; also not in the paper's text proof
Location: main.tex Prop 4 ("Prop 3 set" in label prop:set) and proof; docs/08 Prop 4 heading.
(a) Mechanism wrong. The paper says point identification "can only arise from coincident breakpoints". Coincident breakpoints make more multipliers free (p_k and q_j both range over intervals), i.e. they enlarge, not shrink, the feasible set. They cannot create point identification. 
(b) What actually can give a point: knife-edge ties in which the inequalities pinch. Counterexample (checked numerically): K=1, d=0.5, c=0, alpha=1, linear cost. Period 1 has u>0 with observed D=0: optimality requires omega^+ <= 1; period 2 has observed D=u>0: requires omega^+ >= 1. Together omega^+ = 1, a point. But at omega^+ = 1 F_t is flat on [0,u] (F(0)=F(u)=F(0.7)=1.5 in the example) so period 1's D=0 is one of a continuum of optima; this is exactly the flat-optimum case of docs/08 Section 5. So non-genericity arises from omega_k = lambda (weight equal to the unit adjustment cost) or sums of weights equal to 1, not from breakpoint coincidence. Note that with the paper's strict-optimality margin (open conditions) such pinching is excluded.
(c) Full-dimensionality is not proved. Argument that works: the conditions at a unique (strict) minimiser are open conditions in omega (strict left/right derivative inequalities); the true omega satisfies them strictly iff the minimisers are strict, hence the identified set contains an open neighbourhood of the true omega (intersected with omega>=0) and is full-dimensional; it is a polyhedron because states are fixed by (alpha,c,d,D*_t). The paper's proof only says "unless breakpoints coincide". Replace by the above, assuming strict optimality of every observed decision.
(d) The polyhedron is conditional on fixed (alpha, c, d). The jointly identified set in (alpha,c,d,omega) is a union of polyhedra over cells of the arrangement of kinks (non-convex); the abstract/Section 3 sentence "weights are only set-identified" is fine, but "the set of weights consistent with any sample is a polyhedron" must say "for fixed (alpha,c,d)". Also (alpha,c,d) may themselves only be set-identified.
(e) "By Proposition 1, D*_t is a breakpoint" - see Issue 2(d); requires uniqueness of the minimiser.
(f) "at least one of sigma, p_k, q_k ranges over an interval" is correct only if the observed decision is a breakpoint; add that the interval multipliers yield two-sided inequalities (e.g. D*=u_k>0: sum_A omega^+ - sum_B omega^- <= 1 <= sum_A omega^+ - sum_B omega^- + omega_k^+).
(g) The sets are in general unbounded (only lower bounds on some omegas); say so, since "bounds per weight" is promised in docs/08.
(h) The title in docs/08 ("generically never point-identified") is stronger than the proof; with a strictness assumption it is true, but the claim should be a proposition with the explicit statement: "if every observed D_t is the unique minimiser, the identified set of omega for fixed (alpha,c,d) has nonempty interior; hence no omega is point-identified."

### 6. [minor] The set 'unchanged policy' and 'at D* strictly above' definitions of state are used interchangeably (Props 4-5)
The text calls goals "strictly above the band" in the proofs; define once: state of goal k at the decision D, A_t = {k: u_k > D}, B_t = {k: l_k < D}, on edge {u_k=D} or {l_k=D}. (The design notes use nested binaries a (on-or-above), s (strictly above), l (on-or-below), z (strictly below); the right/left-derivative formulas in docs/08 Section 5 are correct only with that nesting - I verified: right derivative counts goals with D<u_k (s) and D>=l_k (l), left counts D<=u_k (a) and D>l_k (z). State the nesting in the paper.)

### 7. [verified, with refinements] Section 4: continuity of the decision loss under quadratic cost
Claim: correct. F_t(D;theta) is jointly continuous (locally Lipschitz) in (D,theta) for omega>=0 and 1-strongly convex in D uniformly in theta, so the unique minimiser D*_t(theta) is a continuous (indeed locally Lipschitz) function of theta; hence |D_obs - D*(theta)| is continuous. Numerical check: perturbing (alpha, omega) by up to 1e-3 gave |dD*|/|dtheta| <= 2.04. Refinements: (i) needs omega >= 0 (the model with negative weights is nonconvex and the claim fails); (ii) continuity holds in (alpha,c,d,omega) but tau = c/alpha, b = d/alpha blow up as alpha -> 0 (the bootstrap observation), so continuity is in the scaled parameters only; (iii) the loss is continuous but not differentiable and typically multimodal, so continuity only licenses derivative-free methods, not convergence to a global minimum; (iv) the contrast statement should be added: under linear cost D*(theta) is set-valued/discontinuous at ties (omega=1) which is why the margin is needed; the paper says this but not that the quadratic result is the reason a margin is unnecessary. The paper does not give a proof; one line (strong convexity) suffices.

### 8. [minor] Section 4: O(K 3^K) evaluation of the optimum
Claim: the candidate set {u_k, l_k} U {sum_{A} omega^+ - sum_{B} omega^- : (A,B) disjoint} does contain the minimiser. Reason: F_t' is strictly increasing; the minimiser is either a kink (a breakpoint) or an interior point of a smooth piece where F'=0, i.e. D = s(A,B) for the state (A,B) of that piece, and a state assigns each goal one of three values (above / inside / below), so at most 3^K combinations. Since every candidate is a feasible real number and F_t is convex, taking the argmin of F_t over all candidates (O(K) per evaluation) returns the true minimiser. Verified numerically against a 600,001-point grid on 300 random instances, K<=4, all-negative and mixed-sign (with sg), no failures (gap < 1e-4).
Issues: (a) it is exponential for no reason: F_t' is increasing with upward jumps at the 2K breakpoints, so sorting the breakpoints and scanning (cumulative slope sums) finds the minimiser in O(K log K) - this matters for K>3 and for the stated "scalable" algorithm. Replace O(K 3^K) with O(K log K) (or note that 3^K enumerations are only a simple upper bound). (b) Candidate sets need 4^K if d_k<0 is allowed. (c) The paper's phrase "breakpoints and stationary points" omits that stationary points are indexed by *states*, not by ordered intervals; and under quadratic cost 0 is no longer a candidate (except as a stationary point of the all-inside state, which is covered by the empty sum). (d) docs/08 Section 8 says linear-cost enumeration is O(TK) per evaluation; it is O(T K^2) naively (2K+1 candidates times O(K) evaluation) or O(T K log K) with sorting.

### 9. [minor] Eq. (1)-(2), definitions
- eq. (1) has no lambda; docs/08 normalises lambda = 1. Say so.
- "g_kt(D) = alpha_k x_kt - D - c_k" valid only for beta<0 (see Issue 2).
- (y-tau-b)_+ with b_k>=0 is required, as is w^pm>=0; state it.
- The claim in the abstract "a change of variables makes all optimality conditions linear in the unknowns" holds only for the kink positions given states; the optimality conditions involve products omega x state, which the paper linearises with big-M (Section 4). Say "linear given the discrete states".

### 10. [minor] Remark after Prop 5 ("moves in steps of size omega_k while goal k stays outside its band and stops at the band edge")
Correct for K=1 (D* = min(omega^+, u)); with K>=2 steps are sums of omegas of all goals currently outside, and the 'stop' happens when any goal reaches an edge, which may be at a value other than the edge of goal k. State "for K=1 or when other goals are inside".
