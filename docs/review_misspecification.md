# Review: Misspecification theorem and "Model adequacy" section (paper2/main.tex)

Scope: thm:misspec and thm:cons (main.tex 183-196), sec:adequacy (260-269), code/misspec_placebo.py, code/model_comparison.py, results/misspec_placebo.csv, results/model_comparison.csv, results/aid_exact.csv, paper2/tab_placebo.tex, paper2/tab_mcomp.tex. Checks are in /tmp/review3/ (a.py, b.py, c.py, d.py).

Bottom line: the theorem is essentially correct but under-stated, and its practical consequence (the argmin set can be large) is not drawn. The simulation is implemented as described and the table matches the CSV. The conclusions are over-stated: the alarm is never calibrated or applied to real data, and the real-data text and table disagree (5 vs 6 of 23).

## Issues

### 1. [major] Theorem "Misspecification": the cost function and the integrability conditions are not stated
Location: thm:misspec statement and proof.
Problem: thm:cons fixes C(D)=D^2/2, which is what makes D*_theta continuous (steps ii-iii use strong convexity). thm:misspec says "Theta as in (B3)" but not that C is quadratic. Under linear cost D* is set-valued or discontinuous, so the theorem would be false as stated for that case. The condition E|D_t|<infinity is stated, but what is actually used is: pair (x_t,D_t) jointly stationary and ergodic, E|x_t|<infinity (for the Hölder constant L_F(x), linear in |x|), and E|D_t|<infinity (envelope |D_t|+sum of max omega). These are present but the proof just says "steps (ii)-(iii) use neither the model equation nor (B2)", leaving the reader to check, including that the dominated-convergence step needs only the envelope. Also, "minimisers" exist only because Q_T is continuous on compact Theta; say so. The result is also not what the exact algorithm computes (it searches data-dependent ranges for e and omega; see review_theorems.md item 8). The paper notes this in the Monte Carlo text but the theorem does not.
Fix: state C(D)=D^2/2 explicitly. List "(M1) joint stationarity/ergodicity of (x_t,D_t); (M2) E|x|,E|D|<infinity; (M3) = (B3) without theta_0 in Theta". Write two lines of the proof (continuity, envelope, bracketing, Q_T uniform, argmin inclusion) rather than a back-reference. Cite a stationary-ergodic ULLN, not Newey-McFadden Lemma 2.4 (iid; already flagged in review_theorems.md).

### 2. [major] The minimiser set Theta* can be very large, and the paper never says so; the placebo result is exactly this phenomenon
Location: thm:misspec and the paragraph after it ("pseudo-true parameters"); also Theta* is called "pseudo-true parameter set" without noting non-uniqueness.
Problem: Theta* is well defined (nonempty by compactness and continuity, and closed). dist(theta_hat_T, Theta*)->0 is the right conclusion, and point convergence cannot be claimed. But for an L1 criterion Theta* is the set of conditional-median projections and is often huge. In the coin DGP the population median of Delta is 0, so E|Delta-a| is minimised at a=0 and Theta* = {theta: D*_theta(x)=0 a.s.} = all (tau,b) with omega=0, or a band covering the support of x. That is a set with nonempty interior in tau, so tau_hat_T is arbitrary within it (set by enumeration tie-breaking in exact_k1). The paper's observation that the "tau-set width is 3.8 units" and "IQR 1.12" for the coin flip is therefore not just a finite-sample symptom; it is the population non-identification the theorem allows. Also: whenever "hold" is the best predictor (the paper's own real-data finding for 12 of 23 donors and 11 of 14 banks, see item 8), the pseudo-true set is the degenerate "do-nothing" set and no tau is revealed. The text "the aspiration band and steps ... that best predicts the decisions" glosses this.
Fix: add a remark: Theta* is a median-projection set, may contain a continuum (e.g. omega=0), and convergence of the point estimate holds only if Theta* is a singleton (give sufficient condition: as in thm:cons iv, or E over a neighbourhood of Q strictly convex). Say that the data-dependent reported tau-set is the finite-sample analogue. Reference the coin DGP as the illustration.

### 3. [minor] "Pseudo-true parameter" and continuity of D*_theta in theta: correct, but note the boundary
Verified: continuity of D*_theta in theta follows from 1-strong convexity of F_t and Lipschitz dependence on theta; the Hölder-1/2 constant is valid. Boundary issue: omega^±=0 and alpha=alpha_min boundaries are inside Theta (compact), fine, but identification (thm:cons iv) fails exactly there; one sentence suffices.

### 4. [major] Placebo: the "alarm" is not calibrated, and a nontrivial share of coin-flip series look confident
Location: sec:adequacy, placebo paragraph; tab_placebo.tex.
Numbers verified against the CSV (all four rows match: tau medians 2.00/2.13/2.01/1.99, IQR 0.01/1.12/0.10/0.08, gain 78/3/66/70%, width 0.02/3.83/0.07/0.05, beats hold 100/22/100/100, beats PA 98/30/0/4). Using the CSV and, for more precision, 300 fresh coin replications (c.py, own seeds), the coin-flip DGP produces a "confident-looking" result more often than the text suggests:
- 50 reps in CSV: 5/50 (10%) have a tau-set width < 1; all 5 have tau_hat inside the true band [1.5,2.5] (the narrowest, width 0.15, has gain 26% and tau 1.65, OOS beats hold and PA). 3/50 have gain > 5% and width < 1; 1/50 has gain > 10% and width < 0.5.
- 300 fresh reps: width < 1 in 12.7%; gain over hold > 5% together with width < 1 in 11.7%; gain > 10% in 5.3%; gain > 10% and width < 1 in 3.7%; GP beats hold out of sample in 17.7% (22% in CSV), beats PA in 24%; gain > 10% and OOS win over hold in 3.3%.
So the alarm detects the coin DGP in roughly 90% of series on width, and 85-95% on gain/OOS depending on threshold, i.e. a false-confidence rate of about 4-13% depending on thresholds. That is fine as a result, but the paper reports only medians, which hide it, and never states a decision rule or its size. Also the gain over hold is mechanically >= 0 because hold is nested (omega=0), so "3%" needs a null reference; the useful statement is "the 95th percentile under the coin null is 10.6%".
Further problems with the diagnostics: (a) the width uses a 2% relative loss tolerance, so it is scale dependent: in the gp DGP the loss is near the noise floor and the 2% band is tiny in absolute terms, while for the coin the profile is flat; the width is also truncated by the search window [min x - 0.3, max x + 0.3] and scales with the spread of x, so "3.8 units" is not comparable across series (report width / sd(x) or use an absolute tolerance tied to the noise). (b) tau_pa is NaN for 46/50 coin series (slope not negative), so no PA target is shown; fine, but say it.
Fix: report distributions (quantiles, and rates at stated thresholds) of gain, width/sd(x) and OOS wins under the coin null; propose a rule (e.g. "report a target only if gain > 10% and width < 1 sd(x)") and report its false-positive rate (about 4%) and power; state that roughly 1 in 25 coin-flip actors would still receive a tight target.

### 5. [major] Real-data series are not screened with the same diagnostics; many fall inside the coin-flip null range
Location: sec:adequacy (the two paragraphs are not connected) and Discussion ("When to trust an estimate").
Problem: I computed the in-sample gain over hold for the 23 aid donors with the exact solver (d.py). Values: AUS 7.6%, CHE 7.1%, DEU 3.2%, GBR 1.6%, IRL 6.2%, KOR 6.4%, ESP 8.8%, ITA 8.9%, CAN 9.6% and others up to 28% (PRT), median about 11%. Under the coin null the 50th/90th/95th percentiles are 2.9/7.9/10.6%, so about 9 of 23 donors are below the coin null's 90th percentile (and several below the 95th). The placebo section's own alarm applied to real donors therefore flags a large share of them. Yet the paper then says the claims "concern the recovered aspiration levels", and uses six of them (LUX, SWE, DNK, NOR, NLD, GBR) as validation. Several of those (NLD 11.8%, DNK 12.0%, SWE 22%) pass, but this was not shown. Decisions are not "mostly zero" (share of zero changes 2-28%, median about 11%), so the strength of hold is not due to zeros; it is because |Delta| is small and noisy.
Fix: add gain-over-hold and tau-set width to the real-data table (aid_exact.csv has set2 but not gain), and mark donors that fail the placebo-calibrated rule; drop or flag GBR, DEU etc. in any target claim.

### 6. [minor] Placebo DGPs: implemented as described, but the paper omits the details, and seeding is not reproducible
Verified: gp DGP is band [1.5,2.5], cut 0.3, raise 0.4, Laplace 0.03 (X.h(s,1,2.5,1.5,0.3,0.4)) with D=-Delta; coin takes values {-0.3,0,0.4} (hold MAE 0.233, median Delta 0); partial uses 0.3(2-s)+Laplace 0.03; qp uses clip((2-s)/3,-0.35,0.35)+Laplace 0.03. The state is AR(1) with coefficient 0.7 and shock sd 0.6 around 2 plus the feedback s<-s+d. Algebra of the QP closed form: d/dDelta [(x+Delta-2)^2+2Delta^2] = 2(x+Delta-2)+4Delta=0 gives Delta=(2-x)/3, convex so the clip to |Delta|<=0.35 is exactly the box-constrained minimiser. Correct.
Problems: (a) the paper does not report noise (Laplace 0.03), state process, coin support or T beyond "60", so it cannot be reproduced from the text; (b) rng = default_rng(hash((kind,rep)) % 2**32): hash of a tuple containing a str is salted per interpreter (PYTHONHASHSEED); I confirmed two interpreters print different hashes for ('coin',1). The CSV cannot be regenerated bit-for-bit; use a fixed integer seed per (kind index, rep); (c) 50 replications give Monte Carlo SE of about 6 points on the percentages (22%, 30%, 4%); use 200-500; (d) the "qp" DGP is the same kind of object as partial adjustment with a clip (slope 1/3), and is a quadratic-deviation goal program with a step bound; calling it "other goal-seeking behaviour" is fair, but its tau close to 2 is by construction (symmetric behaviour about 2), so "revealed target close to the reference" is a weak test of anything. A better misspecified-but-informative DGP would have an asymmetric or state-dependent reference.

### 7. [minor] Out-of-sample split and PA benchmark in the placebo
The split (first 42 for training, last 18 for testing, per series) is fair in the sense that all models are trained on the same 70% and evaluated on the same 30%, and PA is fitted by LAD (right loss for MAE evaluation), which is an appropriate benchmark. But: hold needs no estimation and the GP has 4-5 free parameters and a flat profile under the coin, so GP loses by overfitting in expectation; with 18 test points per series the per-series win indicator is noisy (the coin medians are 0.248 GP vs 0.250 PA vs 0.244 hold; in 12 of 50 coin series GP and PA tie exactly because both predict the same constant), and ties count as "GP does not beat". Say ties are counted against GP, or report the mean difference with a CI. "GP beats PA 98% when GP is true" is partly the DGP having sharp kinks; fine. "PA beats GP in 96-100%" for partial and qp is again the DGP being PA (the qp row is PA with clip, fitted by linear LAD which is close to exact).

### 8. [major] Real-data text and table disagree; "best model" counts include ties
Location: text "5 of 23 donors and 3 of 14 banks" vs tab_mcomp.tex "6/23" and "3/14".
Verified from model_comparison.csv (b.py): the table's GP-best column is computed by idxmin over (gp, pa, hold, medchg), which returns the first column (gp) on ties. Aid: GP weakly best in 6 donors (AUS, CAN, IRL, JPN, NOR, NZL), strictly best in 5 (all but AUS, whose GP error 0.018947 equals hold exactly). So the text (5) matches the strict count and the table (6) includes the AUS tie. Central banks: weakly best 3 (GB: all four models identical at 0.0278; IL: tie with hold; MX: strict), strictly best only 1 (MX). So "3 of 14" is mostly ties. In aid, GP is worse than hold for 12/23 and in banks worse than hold for 11/14 (median GP/hold error ratio 1.01 for aid, 1.25 for banks). Means in tab_mcomp.tex match the CSV (aid 0.056/0.052/0.049/0.050; banks 0.481/0.413/0.380/0.380; defence 0.183/0.176/0.191/0.170).
Also the text claims "always holding has the lowest average error for aid and central banks": true for aid (0.0486 vs medchg 0.0497); for banks hold and medchg tie at 0.380 (median change is 0 in most banks), so say so. For defence the best average is the median-change model, not hold (not claimed, ok).
Fix: count strict wins, report ties separately ("GP best or tied with hold in 6/23"), and make text and table agree.

### 9. [major/minor] Real-data comparison fairness
(a) Splits: last 30% of each series, test sizes 9-19 for aid (median 19) and 10-32 for banks. MAE differences between models are tiny (aid 0.049 vs 0.056) and no test is applied (Diebold-Mariano or a paired bootstrap across units); the conclusion "GP does not predict better" is plausible but a "5 of 23" count at these sample sizes is noise-level. (b) The Taylor-rule benchmark for banks is a LAD fit on the full regressors, but the GP is the multistart heuristic Q.fit_hybrid (seed 0), which the paper itself says is up to 5% above the optimum on real data and sometimes changes conclusions; the bank GP is therefore not the exact optimum and may be handicapped (the opposite direction is not possible: LAD is exact). State this. (c) Defence is cross-section CV over allies within a period (single shuffle with seed 0), not time-series, so it is a different test; 2 periods are averaged as "2 units"; the 2022-2025 gain is across 123 observations from few years, so errors are correlated within year. (d) Hold is "trivially strong" because Delta has small variance, not because zeros dominate; the paper should say hold is hard to beat when |Delta| is noise-dominated. (e) The structural-break explanations (Sweden leaving its 1% goal in 2023, Netherlands lowering its goal in 2012) are not tested: the Netherlands goal change is inside the training window, so it affects the fitted tau, not just the out-of-sample error.

### 11. [minor] Conclusion wording

## Verified correct
- Theorem structure: steps (ii)-(iii) of thm:cons use only continuity of D*_theta in theta (quadratic cost), the envelope |D*|<=sum of max omega, a bracketing ULLN under ergodicity and compactness; they do not use B2 or the model equation. thm:misspec's proof is valid given those, and dist to Theta* (closed, nonempty) is the correct set-valued conclusion.
- Q(theta) = E|D - D*_theta(x)| is continuous; the Hölder-1/2 constant (2 sqrt(L_F ||dtheta||)) is valid.
- QP closed form and the algebra clip((2-x)/3, -0.35, 0.35).
- DGP implementations match the paper's description (coefficients, steps, noise as in the code); table_placebo numbers equal the CSV; tab_mcomp means equal the CSV; GP-vs-PA implied targets equal the CSV.
- Out-of-sample split is the same for all models; LAD is the correct fit for MAE.
- Python checks run: a.py (CSV recomputation of every table cell and coin-flip diagnostics), b.py (strict/tied best counts), c.py (300 fresh coin replications, own seeds), d.py (in-sample gain over hold for 23 donors vs coin-null quantiles).
